It’s not easy, having the holy curse of prophecy. I sit in the lotus position on my mountain, high above the clouds, trying to pick out some helpful and practical prophecies to bring back down the mountain for you. Meanwhile the entire future of the universe crashes continually around my ears. Since you’re not a prophet, you have no idea what it’s like. Imagine having to pick out the one voice saying something interesting in an entire football stadium of people whispering nonsense like ‘OnlyFans for pets’ and ‘They’re going to come out with a new flavour of Fray Bentos pie, it’s a sushi flavoured pie with lashings of rich onion gravy’ and ‘Noel Fielding necrophile allegations’ and ‘Asteroid impact, millions dead.’

What makes this worse is that some of you people have started climbing up the mountain while this is happening, just to taunt me about my [supposedly failed prophecies](https://samkriss.substack.com/p/prophecies-for-2025). I’m floating through the infinity of things yet to come, and meanwhile some cross-eyed rustic keeps poking me in the ribs with a stick. Hey mister, he says, listen here mister big prophet, you said that ‘the second Donald Trump presidency will be dominated by a kind of apolitical populism, directed more against seed oils and xenoestrogens than imports, migrants, or the liberal classes,’ and guess what, he’s just put tariffs on the whole world. Guess you must be feeling pretty stupid now, huh? In fact, over the course of the last year there’s been a constant pilgrimage of gurning slime-farmers wending their way up my mountain, coming to tell me exactly how apolitical the second Donald Trump administration has been. Hey mister prophet, they say, the Department for Homeland Security is posting sonnenrad edits. Hey mister prophet, government cronies keep making straight-arm salutes. Hey mister prophet, Charlie Kirk tried to smug at some libs in Utah and one of them shot him through the fucking neck, right as he was talking about gang violence statistics mister prophet, how’s that for apolitical, whaddya think about that?

It’s exhausting, having to live among people trapped in time. All you can see is the present that’s right in front of you; I’m being sucked off by aliens in the twenty-eighth century. Yes, Donald Trump put tariffs on the entire world, including an island inhabited solely by penguins. What happened next? Right now, the US is selling its most advanced chips to China, and until 2027 the tariff on semiconductors is a big fat zero. This has been the pattern all year: Trump swings his fists about, trying to look like he’s engaged in some kind of genuine political contention, but there’s nothing there. The murder of Charlie Kirk was supposed to be his Reichstag Fire, the beginning of an all-out life-or-death struggle against the left. What actually happened? Three months on, Kirk’s widow is leaping around in a sparkly costume at TPUSA AmericaFest. The man’s cultural legacy consists of an AI-generated song called _We Are Charlie Kirk_, the ‘kirkification’ meme, which is where you replace a celebrity’s face with Kirk’s, and the word ‘lowkirkenuinely’ (lowkey+kirk+genuinely). Everyone who still remembers the man takes his death as a piece of fundamentally apolitical kitsch. It’s already retro: fun but tacky 2025iana. This is what will happen to you if you devote your life to politics in a fundamentally apolitical world. You will be a joke when you die. Meanwhile, Trump is inviting Zohran Mamdani to the White House, gazing up in admiration at this handsome young socialist, wonderful guy, we all want the same things. I prophesied that there would be zero political events in 2025, and lowkirkenuinely I was right.

Absolutely everything else I prophesied has also come to pass. I said that in 2025 AI video would finally become indistinguishable from reality, and as a result ‘there will never be another interesting AI video again.’ It’s now possible to flawlessly imitate anyone doing anything, but somehow over the last twelve months nothing remotely newsworthy has come of this terrifying new power. The closest was an AI-generated Christmas advert released by McDonald’s in the Netherlands. The gist of the thing was the Christmas is awful, so you may as well spend all of December in McDonald’s, eating one hamburger after another, until it’s over. There was not a single shot that couldn’t have been just as easily generated by pointing a camera at an object and filming it. Unknown trillions have been invested in this technology, all to reproduce something that already exists. ‘At the very most,’ I wrote, ‘AI will replace some forms of B-roll. It will be the least consequential technology of your life.’ I was right. I prophesied the return of ‘Manbooks: books for men,’ books that aren’t obsessed with therapeutic interiority, now you can’t walk down the street in London without seeing someone ostentatiously tote around a copy of _Flesh_ by David Szalay. (The next step is for someone to write a book in which something actually _happens_, but that might be beyond our abilities at the moment.) Most prophetically of all, I said that as Twitter’s cultural cachet waned, it wouldn’t be replaced by Bluesky or Threads or Substack, but _the real world_. Not in the sense that everyone would put aside all this frothing online hysteria and just focus on their actual lives—I meant that instead of trying to whip up cancel mobs on social media, people would just start physically attacking each other. ‘As all the other Twitter methadones stop working, expect an upswing in bizarre and unaccountable acts of public violence and private revenge.’ The Charlie Kirk assassin scratched a bunch of tweets into the bullets he fired. ‘If you read this, u r gay lmao.’ This is my curse. I’m always right.

Anyway, I have once again returned from the mountain with a bundle of prophecies for the year ahead. Last year’s were generated with astrology, and the year before’s with Tarot; this time I’ve been reading the tea leaves. Here is the shape of your next twelve months:

Tasseography is fun: less like reading Tarot cards, and more like reading dreams. You have to distill the shapes yourself out of the general psychic gloop, and every guide to their meanings is provisional and contingent. What does this look like to you? I’ll tell you what it looks like to me. I see the big blob on the left as a prawn, with its square head bristling with antennae, turning back to look over its tail as it jealously clutches its pale brood of eggs. Crustaceans in tea leaves are supposed to represent opportunism, faithlessness, someone that will betray you. But what stands out to me is the prawn’s posture, bent over its own tail. This year will be a year of reversals, things going in precisely the opposite way to the one you thought. A logic that stretches so far in one direction that it suddenly recoils into its opposite. This is your 2026.

#### Obesity is back

GLP agonists—that’s ozempic, wegovy, grey-market Chinese retatrudite, etc—are a class of drug that can, as far as we can tell, fix absolutely everything that’s wrong with you. They will make you thin. They will help you stop drinking, stop gambling, stop taking heroin, stop having sex with evil people, and stop listlessly scrolling on your phone all night until you’re numb enough to sleep. You will no longer experience gender dysphoria. You will become smarter, and also happier. You will experience improved powers of memory. Your heart, liver, and kidneys will all function more powerfully. The drugs can reverse some of the major signs of ageing and will almost certainly increase your lifespan. It’s possible that you will become immortal. We don’t know yet. It hasn’t been studied. But it’s entirely possible that taking ozempic will turn you into a god. You will be a gorgeous, ageless, smart and skinny god, and as long as you keep injecting yourself with the stuff you will never die. You can get it on the NHS for £9.90 a month.

A lot of people are very upset about this. If you can just fix everything with a simple injection, the world is out of balance. Somewhere, there has to be a price. A portrait in the attic. In a decade or two, the ozempic users need to start bursting out in novel and surprising cancers. When they breed, these gorgeous ageless smart and skinny gods need to give birth to something monstrous, apelike, froglike, forked tongue mewling, the dumb festering animal they thought they’d transcended, bursting gorily out the womb with razor-sharp claws. And maybe that will happen. I’m not a pharmacologist. But I don’t think it needs to; the intended effect is enough.

GLP agonists work by interrupting the reward systems that produce compulsive behaviour. Emotional eaters end up joylessly mashing up material in their mouths when they remember they need the stuff to live, but not deriving any particular pleasure from the process. Gamblers notice that they’ve just let a tenner vanish into the slot machine for no good reason, and decide to call it a night. In a way, these drugs have changed nothing. I’ve been saying for years now that our problem is no longer not getting the things we desire, but not knowing how to desire anything at all. The utopians of the last century wanted a world without want, and we got it. Peptides are just a crude literalisation of that thesis. The future is a Buddhist paradise of jabbering, emaciated, undesiring arahats. One great global _Wicked_ press tour.

Except it isn’t, because the horrible side effects these people might really regret aren’t the ones you read on the label, but the ones you read about in _Distinction_ by Pierre Bourdieu. Once being gorgeous ageless smart and skinny is universally available, it’ll no longer represent any kind of cultural capital, and the only thing that can shock us out of the current doldrums of desire is if asceticism becomes déclassé. Right now, too much overt sexuality is considered vaguely tacky, but not for long. Nothing more valuable in a porndead world than the hunger in the eye, the violent propulsion towards life and death. Stupidity too: isn’t there something deeply extravagant about stupidity? Most of all, though, if everyone is immortal, then death becomes a luxury product. Right now, hyper-wealthy fintech lizards are competing to retire as early as possible, thirty, twenty-five; before long they’ll be competing to die. Bryan Johnson will be smoking two packs a day. It takes a lot of money to be bored with existence, and dying is the most sumptuous and extravagant thing imaginable. It also requires a massive quantity of cultural competence to do it right. You can’t get ChatGPT to do it for you. You can’t learn it in some cram school for nerds. There are no second chances. You can only do it once.

These things will take time. In 2026, though, semaglutide will do what a decade of body positivity couldn’t, and make obesity fashionable. Not _any_ obesity, obviously. Doctors disagree, but there are clearly two ways of being dangerously overweight. There’s the spreading shapelessness that comes from eating lots of deep-fried snacks or frankly anything delivered to your door, food that comes in styrofoam and booze from plastic bottles, blasting your brain with short-form video content while you joylessly ingest. This is entirely different from the taut spherical shape you can achieve if you’re actually hungry.

There is a way of being fat that requires you to regularly eat a dozen oysters if they’re in season, a smoked eel or two, and obviously crabmeat, sucked wetly out the creature’s joints, maybe just a few plump lamb’s livers glazed with Madeira, a veal brain croquette, some great sluglike kidneys in a mustard sauce, a pig’s foot stuffed with sweetbreads and morels, maybe, why not, a jar of rabbit rillettes with cornichons and buttered toast, before the main dish, a great wodge of game pie, thick flakes of buttery pastry, steaming purple melange of terrified animals with juniper berries and little fragments of shot, served alongside pommes dauphinoise and haricots verts gently glossed in butter, and once it’s done an oloroso sherry, half a dozen rum babas doused in a pint of cream, and a crumbly pile of Stilton alongside a smaller mound of wrinkling decorative grapes, the whole thing washed down with three or four bottles of good claret and concluded with a fernet digestif. If you feel like you’re going to burst or be sick, you’re doing it wrong. This is not a special occasion; this is Tuesday. You should be able to play a full game of tennis afterwards. Do it right, and you will be rewarded with the most desirable body of 2026.

#### Britain becomes the world’s first censorship superpower

As everyone now knows, there is no such thing as freedom of speech in the UK. Instead, the police have all decided to reconstitute themselves as an unofficial volunteer force of Twitter moderators. They make around 12,000 arrests a year, or thirty a day, for posting-related offences; there’s also the baffling category of Non-Crime Hate Offences, which is where no one’s broken the law at all but representatives of the state still show up at the homes of miscellaneous terves, sternly warning them to act more in the spirit of Paddington Bear. The most recent piece of tabloid fodder has to do with a woman called Elizabeth Kinney who had got into some kind of screaming chaotic Scouse-type feud with a friend of hers, which ended in her being sent to the hospital with a skull fracture after a man she knew attacked her. As far as anyone knows, the man hasn’t faced any legal consequences, but Kinney has: while in hospital, she sent of a stream of screaming chaotic texts to her former friend, and in one of them she described her assailant as a ‘faggot.’ Eleven cops burst into her house. According to the prosecutors, the Crown took her case particularly seriously, given the homophobic content of the text message and the ‘greater harm’ the victim suffered from receiving it. Kinney was convicted and sentenced at a magistrates’ court. This is what it’s like here now. Nothing is beneath the dignity of the law. The guy who’d attacked her wasn’t even gay.

The usual move is to blame all this on the unbearable busybodies of the woke left, but the situation is much, much worse than that. It’s everywhere. Case in point: a lot of the people who usually blow their top over this sort of infringement of our ancient English liberties are currently desperate to heap punishment on a man called Alaa Abd el-Fattah, because they don’t like his tweets. Abd el-Fattah is a British-Egyptian writer and political activist, most famous for being in prison. He was first jailed by the Mubarak government; then when it fell he was jailed by the transitional military authority. He was jailed when the Islamists took power in a democratic election and jailed when they were overthrown in a coup. Finally— after a lot of lobbying from successive British governments—his travel ban was lifted and he was allowed to join his family in the UK. At which point it emerged that around 2010, he’d been posting things like ‘I’m a racist, I don’t like white people’ and ‘I’m a violent person who advocated the killing of all zionists including civilians’ and ‘Don’t worry we where only discussing how we’ll take over your town and rape ur women, us terrorists tend to do that.’ He is now probably the single most hated man in Britain.

If you read Abd el-Fattah’s actual published work, it’s clear that he’s a socialist and a humanist, basically an Egyptian Mark Fisher, if a bit more caught up in the enthusiasms of his time. This meant that around 2010, he seemed to think that all the left’s organisational problems could be solved if people stopped philosophising and theorising and all the youth just came out into the squares. Once it became clear just how dead the millennial socialist project really was, he gradually faded into the damp Rebecca Solnitty vocabulary of a defeated left, Believing in Hope and Hoping for Belief. (There but for the grace of God.) But back in 2010, writing stupid tweets about killing all white people was part of that package. This is not _ideal_, and it’s a neat case study in the ways our freedom of speech can be threatened by things far vaster and subtler than ordinary brute censorship, but we all did it. I think mine went something along the lines of ‘white genocide: it’s crunchy, it’s delicious, and it’s what’s for breakfast.’ Nigel Farage claims he’s reported Abd el-Fattah to the police. There’s an increasingly likely possibility that this country will have spent years rescuing the man from a prison in Egypt just so we can put him in one of our own.

In the end, this censorship is rooted deep in the British character. What really offends us isn’t actual violence or cruelty, but anything that looks too much like _making a scene_, causing a fuss, saying things out of turn. A few hundred years ago a young man of means could head out to his plantation in Jamaica for a few years of malarial frenzy, rum and gunpowder, whip cleaving through to the bone, murdering his slaves in the daytime and raping them at night, and then afterwards come home again to dewy England and dance the cotillion with Susanna. But if he dared mention Susanna’s ankles in mixed company, the frowning aunts of Devon would have him hanged.

Today the frowning aunts are section 127 of the Communications Act 2003 and section 1 of the Malicious Communications Act 1988, which make it illegal to broadcast any sequence of words that is _indecent_, _obscene_, _offensive_, _menacing_, or likely to cause _alarm_ or _distress_. The offence is aggravated if you’re motivated by _hate_. This is buttressed by the Online Safety Act 2023, which forces platforms to constantly monitor their sites for anything that might conceivably be harmful to children. As you’ve probably noticed, this is insane. I recently read Dino Buzzati’s 1942 story _Seven Floors_, which is, essentially, about dying, and how little time you have left. It wasn’t particularly indecent or obscene, but it absolutely was menacing, and it caused me a significant amount of both alarm and distress. Heart rate rose, cortisol spiked; I’m sure it shaved half an hour off my life. If a child read it they might find it very traumatic. And there is a kind of hate in there too: a hatred for the emptiness that closes in around our lives, and our stupid smug denial as we let it happen. Theoretically, I could complain to the police about my reading experience, and have everyone involved in translating and publishing _Seven Floors_ arrested and put on trial. This is what sets our system of censorship apart. In other countries, you’ll be punished if you criticise the government, or insult the state religion, or sometimes if you publicly disagree with some official pseudoscience. We don’t do that in Britain. Instead of censoring based on actual propositional content, we’ve banned vast zones of affect. You really can say anything you want. You just have to be _nice_.

Meanwhile, everyone here is incredibly worried about freedom of speech in the US. It’s why last year’s alternative Christmas message was delivered by Jimmy Kimmel, bravely denouncing the Trump regime to country where you can be arrested and put on trial for hurting the feelings of a cartoon bear. But it’s true, things are not great in America either. 2026 will be the year that two competing censorship regimes, the British and the American, finally duke it out for global supremacy. And while I don’t often say this, I think Britain might have the upper hand. Trump’s system is arbitrary and sadistic. Elected officials spend all day being rage-baited on social media and personally handing out deportation orders in response. One day you’re dunking on the soylennial VP, the next you’re blindfolded in an El Salvadorian torture camp. Piddling stuff. Our system is vast, blank, stately, and all-encompassing. Most importantly, it’s primed for export.

The rest of the world has a bizarre, masochistic need to be told what to do by British people. Once it was colonial administrators, tutors, nannies, valets, consultants, celebrity chefs, refining the despotisms of distant desert tribes; now it’ll be our online safety police. This is Britain’s role in a fast-moving twenty-first century: to hector and harry, to bludgeon the rest of the world into being _polite_. This will be a very attractive product on the international markets. If you’re an oil dictator, or a syphilitic dynast, or a golpista in fatigues, you no longer need to eat the PR problems that come with jailing all your dissidents. Just hand them over to impeccably democratic Britain, where we’ll immediately find them guilty of the crime of being mean.

#### Romantasy loses its romance

People like me love to mutter dark mutterings about how literacy is over. Everyone’s brain is on an infinite scroll now; we’re flicking through the world with grubby automatic fingers. Never mind books; most people don’t have the wherewithal to make it through a _sentence_. The average reader will understand less than 10% of a 40-word sentence; if you’re going for 90% comprehension the optimum length is apparently 14 words, which probably explains a lot. But at the same time, people are clearly reading. Book sales dipped a bit around 2009 as smartphone usage took off, but they’ve completely recovered since. Local newspapers are gone forever, TV shows are written with the assumption that the viewer isn’t actually viewing at all, but fiddling around on another smaller screen throughout, but books are doing fine. It’s just that the vast majority of these books seem to be about women having demeaning sex with goblins.

There are books about being given nipple clamps by elves and books about jerking off minotaurs, but one of the biggest novels right now is _Alchemised_ by SenLinYu. The names have been changed for publication, wizards are _alchemists_ or _necromancers_, but it’s still essentially a piece of _Harry Potter_ fanfic. In this version, the baddies win and Hermoine Granger is delivered as a slave to Draco Malfoy, who chains her up, uses invasive psychic magic on her, and repeatedly rapes her until she becomes pregnant. At the end they fall in love. Here’s a sample passage: ‘It was too late. His resonance poured through her. It was as if she were a tapestry. He found the threads of emotion and ripped them out. He didn’t sedate or paralyse her. It was worse, more violating than that. He took away all the things she felt, leaving her mind scrambling, trying to reconcile the dissonance.’

Obviously, it’s not ideal if the most popular form of literature out there consists of graphic descriptions of Winnie the Pooh repeatedly violating Piglet. The usual take on this stuff is that it’s not really literature at all, just straightforward pornography. Terrible writing, characters that aren’t just paper-thin but were already invented by someone else: you’re not here for any kind of _artistic_ pleasure, you’re here to get off on being fucked by wizards in pointy hats. But actually, I don’t think that’s what’s going on at all. The goblins and wizards aren’t an alibi for all the breeding kink torture porn: it’s the other way round. Not so long ago, the people who now read romantasy were doing something different: they were part of the wave of grown adults still reading YA fiction. Great jelly-eyed freaks, thirty years old, babbling about how _hard_ it is to read age-appropriate books, how _joyless_ they all feel, how lacking in the _creativity_ and _wonder_ they got from their media diet of magical dreck for children. They were embarrassing, and widely mocked, and in the end it worked. Romantasy is the result. Surely no one can tell you to stop reading baby books if the magical elves keep having violent kinky sex.

The annoying thing is that the YA readers did sort of have a point. What are your options, if you decide to read real books for grown-ups? Recently, after a year of being hectored into it by practically everyone I’ve ever met, I bought a copy of _On the Calculation of Volume_ by Solvej Balle. It’s amazing, they said, it’s extraordinary, it’s a new frontier in literature. So I read the thing, and it’s a Woman In A Room book. A Woman In A Room book is a book in which a woman is in a room, thinking about things. The gimmick in _On the Calculation of Volume_ is that the woman is in the room because of a kind of _Groundhog Day_ situation where the same day repeats over and over again. So she camps out in the spare room, hiding from her husband for reasons that don’t actually make very much sense, and describing the small banal sounds he makes as he moves innocently around the house. There’s nothing intrinsically _wrong_ with a Woman In A Room book, if you’re good enough to make language do interesting things all by itself. Samuel Beckett’s _Trilogy_ is essentially a Man In A Room book. But a lot of the time, you get the sense that people write these things because they themselves are women in rooms, not doing much, and they genuinely can’t imagine any other subject matter. Solvej Balle is one of those. She is not good enough to make language do interesting things all by itself. Here’s a sample passage: ‘That is why I began to write. Because I can hear him in the house. Because time has fallen apart. Because I found a pack of paper on the shelf. Because I’m trying to remember. Because the paper remembers. And there may be healing in sentences.’ Here’s another: ‘We were living in two different times. That was all. Two times that had flooded their banks. At a place where rivers meet and converge, a kind of temporal Mesopotamia where the Euphrates and the Tigris are merely two different names for water.’ It’s all like this, somehow pompous and plain all at once. Not a single glint of wit or interest. Just this prose like a fart slowly filling all the corners of a large, windowless hall. Horrible. Give me anything other than this. Give me the priapic gnomes! Give me a whole herd of horny minotaurs!

I don’t think literary fiction is dead, but its future is elsewhere. It’s with the romantasy freaks: at least they’re still genuinely interested in something. We’ve already seen that they’re responsive to mockery: once it became uncool to openly read goblin books for babies, they smoothly transitioned to goblin books that were also full of violent sex. But this is starting to pick up a fair amount of mockery as well: once you’ve packed it with smut, how else can you prove that your infantile dreck really is for adults? One option would be goblin books that creatively engage with the language that forms their substance, goblin books that move through words in radical and difficult ways, goblin books that try to find new symbols to represent the unrepresentable depths of goblin subjectivity. There’s no one else that can do it. Don’t be surprised if, by the end of 2026, you’re reading a truly brilliant piece of literary modernism that just happens to feature a sexually sadistic centaur.

#### MAGA goes global

Europeans still have a very strange understanding of the world, in which we assume that we understand it and Americans don’t. Americans are all coon-eating sister-fucking yokels with menacingly blank stares, whose world extends only from one shining sea to the other. Did you know that half of them don’t even own a passport? As if the annual tide that picks us up from Germany and Sweden and deposits us on the beaches of Spain has also somehow turned us all into urbane cosmopolitans for whom nothing human is alien. I think it’s because a lot of us have never actually _met_ very many Americans, which means our understanding of them is coloured by watching Donald Trump win elections, and then imagining the kind of person who would vote for him. But our imaginations are very limited. We don’t understand how even our cartoon American idiot is about to discover the world.

The reason is Trump’s deranged foreign policy. He has occasionally tried to position himself as an anti-war president, and is true that he hasn’t started a war so far and probably never will. He doesn’t have the attention span for war. Under his predecessors the United States was a gangster, operating a global protection racket; under Trump it’s a violent schizophrenic in a train carriage, leaping around with a machete, slapping the terrified passengers around the face, sometimes stroking their hair, warbling mad syllables. You could try to fight him, grab the machete out his hands, but there’s a good chance he’d hack off your head. So you sit, paralysed, and do nothing.

Trump jerks around the world at random, making moves all his predecessors balked at. Threatening war with Canada. Threatening war with Denmark. Generations of neocons dreamed of the day they’d get to bomb Iran; he actually did it. Bombing Nigeria; bombing Venezuela, then kidnapping its President out his bedroom and putting him in a jail cell in Brooklyn. But none of these psycho interventions have actually _done_ anything. No actual policy has been advanced. The Nigeria strike was supposed to be about protecting Christians from religious persecution, but actually hit an entirely unimportant village; it’s possible the Nigerian government used American missiles to go after some ordinary bandits. The Iran raid might have set back the country’s nuclear programme by only a few months. The abduction in Caracas made _no sense at all_. The government has claimed it’s about drug smuggling, but Venezuela supplies at most 1% of the US market; it’s such a flimsy pretence that two of the four charges Maduro faces in court are ‘possession of machineguns’ and ‘conspiracy to possess machineguns.’ Trump keeps talking about oil, but while Venezuela’s reserves are huge, they’re also poor quality, and with oil prices at their lowest point since 2021 the stuff is barely worth refining. These are not acts of war: something stranger, having to do with the world of symbols and desires. The best theory is that Trump was incensed by Maduro dancing on Venezuelan TV; he felt disrespected. A gibbering and a slapping and a stroking of faces. He killed maybe eighty people in Venezuela: if a foreign government stormed a US city and killed eighty Americans for no good reason, half the world would be on fire. But he has the machete, and all anyone else can do is sit quietly and wait for him to be distracted by someone else.

Before the rest of the world does anything to stop him, I think his own voter base might. Being a Trump supporter now involves a constant roving education on the cultures and societies of the world. Back when America fought actual wars, this happened slowly. You had years and decades to learn to hate Iraq. But Trump might make some kind of cackhanded and crackheaded intervention in Cameroon _tomorrow_, and then you need to find out as quickly as possible that there’s a country in Africa where the spirit of Agincourt still lives, and they’re having a low-level civil war between the people who speak English and the people who speak French. And then you’ll need to know why he’s threatening Portugal over the Azores, and why he’s gibbering at the Bhutanese now, and what’s going on in West Papua, and on and on, until finally the diehard MAGA base end up with a profound awareness of the vastness of this world and the splendour of everything within it.

This is dangerous for any nationalist movement, because the grand span of human societies represents the permutations of the human soul, and there is no one in the world who might not find, in some fragment of an alien culture, an image of themselves in all their individuality. One by one, millions of Americans will suddenly discover a strange affinity for the distant countries they’re never quite at war with. A warrior ethos, a kinship structure, some elk-bone pipe or puberty initiation ritual that feels, unaccountably, like home. Maybe they were wrong, maybe it was never America at all. Maybe it’s Uruguay First; maybe it was Kyrgyzstan they wanted to make great again all along.

#### The entire economy will just be insider trading

It’s been a strange year to be a prophet. I sit on my mountain, distilling urgent truths from the winds of prophecy as they surge around my ears, and then I descend to find that you people have all been turning my curse into a game. At the start of 2024, around $100 million was traded on prediction markets every month; now it’s $13 billion. According to Polymarket and Kalshi and the other platforms, prediction markets are financial exchanges that allow you to transform your unique view of the future into a tradable instrument. The idea is that with enough of these trades, we can use the distributed computation mechanism of aggregate market activity to create an almost perfect forecasting machine. If you want to know the likelihood of any future event, just check the markets. Because the trading prices are public, everyone can be their own prophet. This is a fun vision, but it’s slightly undercut by the fact that the vast majority of all trades on these platforms are sports bets. Gambling is illegal in a lot of the US, but if you call your sportsbook a financial exchange for predictions about the future, suddenly it’s fine.

I’m not particularly worried that prediction markets will put me out of a job. They don’t work: of course they don’t work. Polymarket only predicted 67% of markets better than chance, which is unsurprising if you’re not under the delusion that aggregating millions of ignorant opinions will somehow yield the truth. Non-prophets have a notoriously hard time distinguishing the things that _will_ happen from the things they _want_ to happen. This is why people usually bet on the sports teams they support, and why everyone’s ins and outs lists for 2026 went along the lines of ‘IN: reconnecting irl, inviting me to parties, having sex with me. OUT: disconnection, jaded irony, dating apps.’ This is also why you get the phenomenon of depressive realism: people too miserable to want anything are likely to make more accurate predictions about the world. But the people who do try to forecast dispassionately are still insect men with tiny diligent woodpulping minds. They think the profit motive incentivises accuracy; I know that knowledge of the future is a holy madness. Forecasters extrapolate from existing trends; only prophets can count the sudden inversions that make up the substance of history. We are the ones who bellow in the catastrophe: ‘Awake, ye drunkards, and weep; and howl, all ye drinkers of wine, because of the new wine; for it is cut off from your mouth. Hath this been in your days, or even in the days of your fathers?’

This doesn’t mean prediction markets are useless. It’s just that instead of _predicting_ the future, their real function is _making people do things_. These are, after all, a real-time record of millions of people’s dreams and desires, helpfully weighted both by capital and intensity of preference, and you can use it to get rich.

The technical term for this is _insider trading_, and it’s already rampant. Just before the US raid in Venezuela, someone bought tens of thousands of contracts on Polymarket, priced around seven cents, that paid out a dollar if Maduro was removed from power before the end of January. After he was kidnapped, they made nearly half a million dollars. In ordinary markets, this is extremely illegal. In the world that’s coming, it’s the fundamental nature of all economic life. This is the most basic operation. You walk into a coffee shop and make a $5 bet that no one will give you a coffee. The barista takes the opposite side, makes you one, and the contract resolves in their favour. The prediction market coordinates economic activity, but the only actual exchange is in financial instruments, not coffee. Instead of a market in goods, we live in a constant oscillation between different versions of the future.

You can scale up. The government wants to build a bridge; instead of inviting firms to bid for tender they just bet against the bridge being built. If a group of local NIMBYS don’t want it, they can try to make the bridge a less profitable venture by predicting that it will, in fact, happen. Extremely weird things start to crop up the more this system expands. The more unlikely something is to happen, the more money you can get if you _make_ it happen, even if making it happen has no actual benefit to anyone. There is a very low chance that man-eating wolves will roam the streets of Paris like they did in 1450: low enough that it makes a lot of sense to release some. Global instability becomes profitable in and of itself. Eventually this will be priced in, at which point profitability becomes an escalating game of new weirdnesses, individuals trying to engineer bizarre outcomes in ways the distributed aggregate forecasting machine won’t be able to predict. The future will not be boring.

I’m usually sceptical about overhyped new technologies; the reason I think _this_ overhyped new technology might have legs is that it seems to follow a very basic law of history. Marx remarked that all modes of production exist in a kind of larval form within the ones that preceded them, waiting to burst out. He forgot to mention that when this happens, the new mode always takes the form of a _crime_. Pre-agricultural societies often have a particular horror of slavery, which cuts away all of a human being’s human ties and turns them into a transferable asset. Captives must not be put to work: either you adopt them into the clan, or you torture them to death. It’s not that slavery is inconceivable in their world: it would be so _easy_ to do, but as soon as you let it happen your entire kinship society dissolves into a patchwork of hostile classes and property claims. Our name for that collapse is _civilisation_. Similarly, in feudal society usury was both a crime and a sin, punished by excommunication, seizure, sometimes death. The whole process of reinvesting the surplus was the object of a visceral horror: money, which is lifeless, should not reproduce like living things. It’s obligatory to waste the surplus on aristocratic extravagance and war, rather than letting it rot into capitalism. The next mode of production is always something latent in the way we do things, the logical conclusion you’re not allowed to approach. The existing form has to slap a bunch of arbitrary-seeming restrictions on it, because otherwise this new thing will crack through the surface if the world like an eggshell. You can see why Marx thought it might be socialism: trade unions were illegal in Britain for a generation under the Combination Acts, and tightly controlled in most of the rest of the world. But it’s possible that the thing that finally replaces capitalism will grow out of the internal logic of capital itself. Not the international proletariat, but the millions of desperate zoomer gamblers, mouths bulging with zyn, doing insider trading in ways we can’t even conceive, making the number go up as the world crumbles around them. Happy new year.
