_THE STORY SO FAR: By late 2026, Britain was in crisis. Keir Starmer’s government had become so deeply unpopular that it posed an active and ongoing threat to public order. The Prime Minister couldn’t wobble his big gormless head in public without spontaneous lynch mobs forming in his vicinity. It wasn’t even clear exactly what he’d done wrong. People said he didn’t have any ideas. They said he didn’t care what people thought. But he’d had the idea of banning smoking in smoking areas, and when people said they didn’t like that idea he had the idea of not doing it. People didn’t like the idea of children watching online porn, so he’d had the idea of making everyone show a government ID before they could read the word ‘fuck’ on their phones. People were unhappy about immigration, so he’d given a speech about how immigration was actually bad, and then when some other people were unhappy about the speech he immediately walked it back. He had given people everything they wanted and they still couldn’t stand him. It was a mystery. Maybe they were upset about the rain, which had fallen in a grey wash over Britain for more than three hundred uninterrupted days, waterlogging the fields, backing up the sewers, slowly soaking through roofs and into people’s homes, until there wasn’t a patch of dry carpet in the entire country, until all the paintings in the Tate had gone streaky, until all the old incunabula in the British Library had dissolved into mush, until absolutely everyone from the bankers to the tramps was walking around in the faint whiff of damp and mildew. But surely people knew better than to blame him for the weather?_

_Whatever it was, the country was on the edge of something very dangerous. It looked like the next election would be a cataclysm, and rather than hand power over to the maniacs of the right and the imbeciles of the left, the Starmer government bravely decided that the only way to protect British democracy was to trim off all the inefficiencies and radically streamline it for the twenty-first century. Parliament was closed for good; the Palace of Westminster, with its leaking roofs and grotty corridors and general squalid disrepair, was sold off to the Qatar Investment Authority to be turned into a hotel called The Churchill. People were still free to form political parties if they wanted, but there wasn’t much point since nobody would be voting for any of them. Instead, the fate of the country would be chosen through a more familiar mechanism, more in keeping with the lifestyles and experiences of the UK public, and less tainted by the failures of the past: a glossy BBC competition show fronted by Claudia Winkleman._

_Theme music. CLAUDIA WINKLEMAN hobbles out to tepid applause. The studio roof is leaking; here and there small dribbles of stagnant rainwater gently moisten the AUDIENCE, and frankly CLAUDIA doesn’t look fantastic either. These days her fringe covers half her nose. Mascara stripes the puffy cheeks of a waterlogged corpse. Exhausted monotone through her introduction. Twelve weeks ago, we started searching for ordinary Brits with brilliant, non-ideological solutions to help drag the country out of its economic, social, and meteorological doldrums. Tonight, it’s the final of_ Who Wants to Save the UK? _, and there can only be one winner. We’ve already said goodbye to fan favourites like Andy (‘Just hang the lot of them’), Claire (‘Just make everything in the shops free’), and Dmitri (‘Just liberalise the planning system’), and now only four hopeful saviours remain. One of them will be granted total dictatorial power for three years. The other three will have to flee the country before they turn up on the proscription lists. That last bit’s a joke. Nobody in the AUDIENCE laughs._

_She introduces the JUDGES. Lights spin and land on MARY BEARD, JEREMY CLARKSON, and KEIR STARMER, who’s about to resign his elected position for a permanent sinecure on reality TV. As soon as the spotlight falls on KEIR a collective howl of rage bursts out of the AUDIENCE. Out their seats, frothing like animals, scrabbling against the walls of KEIR’s bulletproof acrylic box, desperate to claw out his eyes. Hiss of tear gas. Small yelps of pain as the producers activate some of the more frenzied assailants’ shock collars. Riot police on horseback charge into the studio and stand around bellowing. Through all this KEIR winces the faint wince of a man who’s just found a hair in his salad. On with the show._

_You know our finalists already. They are, in descending order of social status, HAMZA HARAMZADA, RORY FITZWEATHER-REGRETTABLE, FUCHSIA WALLOP, and SAM KRISS._

#### Mass deportations for the elderly

_The first brilliant idea to save the UK comes from HAMZA HARAMZADA a 26-year-old finance lawyer from London. Pink shirt, shiny shoes, shiny forehead. A shiny smoothness everywhere about the man, the gloss of a CGI model awkwardly inserted into reality. He wants to send your granny to a war zone._

There is a dangerous population in this country. They are not like you and me. They don’t work. They don’t contribute to public finances. They hold views that many people consider totally foreign and incompatible with a democratic society, and their numbers are growing. I’m talking, of course, about old people. The old age dependency ratio, the ratio of retirement-age to working-age adults, is nearly a third; in 1960 it was only 18%. The share of the population that pay more in taxes than they collect in benefits is now under 50%. Over the Channel in France, the average retiree now earns more from their pension than the average worker from their salary, and we’re not far behind. The triple lock essentially guarantees that pensions will always grow faster than wages, as an increasingly narrow population of workers are forced to support an increasingly bloated population of idle old codgers. Every other problem in Britain is downstream of this issue. It’s why the government can’t fund decent services for working people. It’s why you can’t find a decent home to live in. It’s why the NHS is overstretched. Who gets ill? Not me. I’ve never been sick in my life. It’s them.

This is also why the social fabric of this country is falling apart. Since this problem first started emerging in the late twentieth century, every successive British government has tried to solve it by importing young people to make up the balance. Care workers, to look after the elderly and incontinent Brits, but also a constant flow of migrant labour in general, as many people as it takes to artificially overheat the economy so the state pensions keep getting paid. We’re living through the results of that now. But it didn’t even solve the problem. What will happen to these new arrivals in a few short decades? They’ll get old, and start collecting pensions themselves. There has to be another way.

I’m not suggesting that this country should abandon the promises it made to its older people. But we need to honour them in a cost-effective way. Instead of trying to arbitrage the international labour market by importing care workers, we could look after our elderly for a fraction of the current cost simply by deporting them all to the Third World.

Here is my vision. Right now, our former colonial subjects in Myanmar, who we all cherish, are undergoing a tragic civil war. They also have miles of gorgeous tropical beachfront, particularly in Rakhine State and Tanintharyi Division, that are almost totally undeveloped, and mostly under the control of local ethnic militia. It should be very easy to convince the Arakan Army or the Mon National Liberation Front, in return for some token recognition or military assistance, to cede a suitable patch of land for us. We’re building a retirement community. We can get Quinlan Terry to design it. I’m picturing Georgian-style townhouses, red phone boxes, old-fashioned pubs on every corner, family butchers, flags and bunting everywhere, and everything clean and tidy, a perfect metropolis-sized recreation of the lost Britain of long ago, but enjoying the balmy warmth of the Bay of Bengal. A haven from the twenty-first century. Meanwhile, the average wage in Myanmar is under £3,000 a year; our new city will provide tens of thousands of good, well-paying jobs for local residents while drastically cutting costs for the British taxpayer.

But the benefits to Britain won’t just be financial. Yes, we’ll be able to drastically cut taxes once all the old people have been shipped off abroad, and yes, the sudden glut of properties on the market will massively reduce the cost of living. Most of all, though, we will reap the benefits of no longer being an old, grey, declining nation. This will be a young country again. The median age will drop from around 40 to about 32, which will make us younger than any time since 1960. Full of vigour, provocative new ideas. You’ll feel it walking down the street, the energy in the air. No more mawkish nostalgia. No more swollen red faces on _Question Time_. Our culture and politics will no longer be held hostage to the whims of an overinflated cohort of sentimental fogies. Maybe you can defer your deportation as long as you keep working, but frankly maybe you can’t. Maybe we’ll have secret police fanning through the shires, dragging your elderly relatives out of the hidden room you’ve built for them and marching them onto a one-way flight to the genocide zones. You won’t miss them really. You’ll be amazed at how little you’ll miss them once they’re gone.

#### Become the 29th state of India

_The second contestant is 63-year-old RORY FITZWEATHER-REGRETTABLE, who used to write pop-history books and now mostly reviews overpriced hotels for airline magazines. Everything about the man is rumpled; shirt, jacket, corduroys, hair, smile. It’s a disguise. Something cold and glinting beneath. He wants to make sure Brexit really does mean Brexit._

It’s been ten years since we voted to leave the European Union, and I don’t know if anyone could claim the experiment’s gone well. Unshackling ourselves from Europe has not made us a globe-girdling mercantile state again. We are not a powerhouse. We are, it turns out, a small, weak country, lost and alone in an age of big, billion-strong heavyweights. We do not command respect on the world stage. It’s got to the point that we’re being bossed around by Mauritius. We are clearly not in a position to tough it out by ourselves. We will have to team up with _someone_.

It’s not unusual to hear Britain described as the unofficial 51st state of the USA. All our culture is either imported from America, or made with one eye anxiously watching the American market. Our foreign policy is dictated from Washington, and our military is practically integrated into the American command structure. We care more about their politics than we do our own. British politicians will sometimes mention some startlingly American principle, like the separation of powers, or the separation between church and state, in a way that suggests they’re not entirely sure what country it is they’re governing. Occasionally it’s tempting to think that we should just give up the pretence, admit that we’re no longer capable of governing ourselves, and turn the country over to the Americans.

In practice, this would be a terrible idea. I think it might well destroy us. If the UK were an American state, it would be the poorest in the Union, poorer than West Virginia, poorer than Mississippi. We would be a charity case. It’s counterintuitive, but joining the US would also make trading with the other 50 states impossible. This is thanks to a century-old piece of legislation called the Jones Act, which states that only American-built and American-flagged ships can carry goods between two American ports. Since all the container ships crossing the Atlantic are built in Japan and Korea, it would instantly make £200 billion of UK exports to America totally illegal. It’s likely that whatever productive industry we still have left would simply pack up and relocate to Arizona.

Luckily, we have other options. America is not the only former colony that can shelter us. All we need to do is accept that when Robert Clive marched his troops into Calcutta in 1757, the fates of Britain and India were permanently intertwined. We gave India a language and a system of government; India gave us a cuisine. The nearly two centuries in which India and Britain formed a political unit were some of the most glorious in British history; the eighty years we’ve been apart have been eighty years of decline. It’s time to come together again.

I can’t think of anything that would be better for British national pride than becoming part of India. We would instantly go from being a distinctly middle-of-the-pack European country, with large swathes that are poorer than Romania or Slovakia, to the single richest state in the biggest country on the planet. The British economy would be nearly as large as the rest of India put together. London would instantly displace Mumbai as India’s economic centre. But I think we’d also find a lot to be proud of in our wider national identity. If we were part of India, iconic British brands like Jaguar Land Rover, Tetley Tea, and Hamleys would no longer be foreign-owned. The UK was the world’s biggest steel-producing country well into the twentieth century; today we’re the only developed nation that doesn’t produce any of its own primary steel at all. As part of India we’d rejoin the top rank of industrial powers. Britain is the only country to have developed an independent space launch capacity, and then given it up. As part of India, ours would be a spacefaring nation once again. Combined with India we’d have the world’s third largest economy, and a military capable of independently standing up to Russia and China. We would loom over Europe like a colossus.

There will have to be a few sacrifices. This wouldn’t be the first time a local monarchy has been integrated into the Republic of India, and the procedure is well-established: like all the other nizams and nawabs, King Charles would have to lose his formal title. Still, he could, like many of them, continue to act as an unofficial Maharaja of Westminster. If you don’t think he’d secretly prefer this, you don’t understand our sovereign. (Incidentally, Charles can, through Cerdic of Wessex, trace his genealogy to the god Odin. Norse-Vedic cognates are a little thorny, but given Odin’s associations with change, suffering, and the underworld, this means that we could probably place the House of Mountbatten-Windsor in the Chandravamsha or lunar dynasty, alongside the ceremonial kings of Jaisalmer, Karauli, and Kutch, plus Lord Krishna and the Buddha.) Similarly, the House of Lords could continue as an unelected Vidhan Parishad, although its official functions would also diminish. We’d have to shed a few other state trinkets, like the Church of England and the BBC. But in return, we’d get our history back.

When India incorporates Britain, it will necessarily have to incorporate our entire past. It’s lately become very fashionable to cock a snook at the British Empire, an empire a good number of my ancestors, incidentally, dedicated their lives to furthering. Certainly mistakes were made in the past, terrible mistakes. But a nation is held together by its history, and we’re now encouraged to view our history with shame. Recent British history shows you where that gets you. Once we become Indian, though, that will change. It will be necessary to finally recognise what was always true: that the British Empire was fundamentally an _Indian_ empire, just as much an indigenous state as Gupta or Vijayanagara. After all, the vast majority of everyone who ever lived under the Empire was Indian. Britain might have projected its power into a few other corners of the world beyond the subcontinent, but then so did the Cholas. It might have been a cruel, extractive, arrogant state, and one ruled by a single caste, but so were all the others. Everyone knows you’re allowed to celebrate belligerent and bloodstained empires as long as they’re in some sense indigenous. No one gets upset at Mongolians for venerating Genghis Khan; no one asks Moroccans to apologise for the crimes of the Almohads. We can be like them. We will no longer be colonised or coloniser, staring at our fellow-creatures across the gulf of a long-vanished history. We can finally shed the colonial guilt that’s made it so hard to take real pride in being British. We can be a proud and glorious nation again. Jai Hind! Bharat Mata Ki Jai!

#### Enfeoff the scroungers

_Slouches forth FUCHSIA WALLOP. She is 38, grew up in a bleak huddled former mill town, works in local government, and is dressed in what clearly used to be a curtain. Face well-scrubbed, hair pulled back, but her teeth are halfway brown. She wants to bolster the ranks of the aristocracy._

Eighty years ago, the working people of this country came together to build a new society out of the ashes of war. We believed that the true measure of a society was not how much land it could conquer, or how many gaudy monuments it could build to itself, but what it did for the poorest and weakest in its ranks. That’s why we set out to make sure that everyone, no matter who they were, could be guaranteed a decent home, and healthcare when they needed it, a good education for their children, and a life lived with dignity.

That’s gone now. This country has changed; we’re not the same people we used to be. I don’t know exactly when the tipping point was, but somewhere in the last fifty years we stopped believing. The welfare state still exists, but the public consent has gone. Instead, it exists as the object of a bizarre collective rage. People who have no idea how big the actual national budget is or how much the government spends are routinely incensed by any suggestion that anyone might be getting assistance from society at large. They are furious, hopping mad, great plumes of steam coming out their ears, because asylum seekers get paid a weekly stipend to cover their basic needs. You know how much it comes to? £9.95 a week. But a tenner a week is too much. They’re incensed, having frothing apoplexies on the floor in their kitchens, legs twitching, eyes rolling back into their heads, because local councils spend 10% of their budgets helping kids with special needs go to school. It’s a lot of money, I’ll admit; billions. But I can’t think of any better thing to do with our society’s surplus. Would you rather have another tedious train line? Would you rather we spent the money on a bunch of wind turbines? More bombs for Israel? I think it would be a profound testament to the goodness of our society if we could all agree that even in difficult times, there’s nothing more important than making sure that every single child can have an education, even if they’ll never use it. There’s a grandeur and a greatness of heart there. But instead, we freak out about it. How small and petty we’ve become.

The most galling thing about all of this, though, is that very few people seem to have the same attitude towards the _other_ class of people who receive big piles of money while not doing any productive work. The entire UK economy now runs on various forms of rent-seeking. The utility companies that don’t actually operate the power stations or water treatment plants, but still get to make a profit every time you pay your bill. The privatised railways. The privatised schools. All their shareholders. Maybe most of all the private landlords, who get to make a tidy income simply by owning a property. It’s not like they actually build any of the homes they let out, and most of them don’t even manage them either; they hand over the day-to-day upkeep of their properties to some rental management company. They don’t really have any function at all. And behind them there’s our entire financial services sector, which no longer lends money for manufacturing, but instead provides loans to aspiring landlords, so they can buy houses that other people then have to pay them to live in. After our brief experiment in capitalism, we’re once again becoming a feudal society, in which rents constantly flow upwards to a class of arrogant idlers.

Which leads me to my solution. If that’s our future, if our welfare state is doomed, then it’s time to accept it, and see if we can’t find a new way to make sure society still has a place for the most vulnerable.

The technical term for what I want to do is _enfeoffment_. Enfeoffment is an agreement in which one person, the seigneur, gives another, the feoffee, tenure to a parcel of land. The last mass enfeoffment in this country was in 1066, following the Norman Conquest. William I, claiming alodial title over the whole of England, confiscated the landholdings of the old Anglo-Saxon owners and redistributed them among the mercenaries and warlords who’d crossed the Channel with him. In turn, the knights subinfeudinated these parcels out to their own followers; the whole complex of ownership was eventually recorded in the Domesday Book. Eventually, this process ended up creating the English aristocracy we have today. That aristocracy still looms large in this country; we can’t seem to stop making TV shows about them and their romantic misadventures. But it’s very small. There are fewer than eight hundred titled hereditary aristocrats in the entire country. Four hundred barons, two hundred earls, a hundred viscounts, so few marquesses or dukes it’s barely worth mentioning them. You could fit the entire peerage in a single Tube train. If we’re going to backslide into feudalism, why not expand it?

I propose abolishing all forms of welfare and replacing them with noble titles. All public housing will immediately be converted into a feudal grant. This will involve creating about two million new baronies, each with a manor and lands consisting of a single one, two, or three-bedroom flat in a tower block. Each resident will be entitled to be addressed with all due dignities, as, for instance, The Right Honourable The Lord John Smith of Flat 18, Chamberlain Place. Obviously the title will also come with rights and incomes, which can effectively replace Universal Credit; people with additional needs, like the long-term disabled, might have to be created a viscount.

This might sound like an ambitious project, and it is. But it might not be as big as you think. There will be a high rate of turnover. An enfeoffment is not the same as a handout. It’s a contract; in exchange for the land, the seigneur gets something in return. Traditionally, it’s loyalty and military service: the feoff has to provide a certain number of armed men for the battlefields. I don’t think it would work if we tried to replace this with some kind of litter-picking, community work, going door to door with a bucket for Red Nose Day. It’s too much like punishment, and we are absolutely not trying to punish people for needing benefits. The point of all this, after all, was to make sure that the most deprived people in society can live a proud and dignified life. Spending half your life in the terror of combat was once considered one of the honours of nobility. Warfare is ennobling. We’re going to have to start a war.

#### Focus on the arts

_The final contestant is a wreck. SAM KRISS is pale as puke, a clammy sheen on his freakishly high forehead with its sad clotting of increasingly sparse hairs. He looks like he hasn’t shaved in a long time, or slept for that matter. There’s yogurt in his beard. He cowers under a kind of protective cape he seems to have made himself, which consists of hundreds of small plastic England flags he’s stitched together. Scabby fingers. Crusty eyes. A man who has visibly lost contact with reality; the unfortunate refuse of our society, doomed to gibber in underpasses, casting piteous looks at the more fortunate as they pass, whining like a diseased street dog. Something should be done for him, but really there’s nothing to be done. Allegedly, he’s a writer. His age is impossible to fathom. He wants kids to do more art in school._

After the Holocaust, there were some people who claimed to be baffled by what had happened, the inconceivability of it all; not that this mass industrial slaughter of human beings had taken place, but that it had been the Germans who’d done it. The Germans, the most refined and poetic people in Europe, full of such tender feelings, the home of high culture, the nation that produced Goethe, Hölderlin, Bach, Schubert… But of course the average German is, and always has been, a brutish, emotionless philistine. A German’s natural habitat is just outside a burning library. Afterwards they will diligently collect the ashes and use them to make industrial dyes. This is who they are; this is what they’re like. God gave them a little spinning cogwheel where the soul should be. The reason Germany produced Goethe, Hölderlin, Bach, and Schubert is that any great artist is always violently reacting _against_ whatever society they find themselves in. In any country, the great artists will always express the direct opposite of the national character.

I’m sure you’ve already noticed all the many ways I’m right about this. The great Russian artists are all spiritualists and mystics, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky, Platonov, Malevich, but actually existing Russians are all deeply cynical and mercenary and totally indifferent to any notion of cosmic justice. All they believe in is screwing everyone else over and not getting screwed themselves. The great Italian artists all make delicate compact little mechanisms in which everything is perfectly composed, from the paintings of Botticelli to the stories of Calvino, but their countrymen all live lives of absolute chaos and squalor; they spend their lives standing around in string vests in their poky little apartments, having screaming arguments with their relatives. American art is ecumenical, world-girdling, Melville, Whitman, while Americans are all deeply parochial and incurious. Latin American writers keep going off on Borgesian or Márquezian flights of fancy precisely because their nations are all composed of the planet’s most terrified and superstitious peasants. Sometimes the effect is so stark that people start to confuse the culture of the artists for the country itself. You think the French are sensuous to the point of cruelty, but really that’s de Sade, Baudelaire, Mirbeau, Gide, and Godard; an actual Frenchman is just a potato-eating park masturbator.

Finally, the English. Somehow, we’ve built a reputation for being gentle, self-effacing eccentrics with a dry, understated wit. Go outside, watch us in the environment we’ve built for ourselves, and see if you find any of that dry, understated wit. We are a country of stupid, ugly, crass, tawdry, arrogant, bigoted morons. The stupidest, ugliest, crassest, tawdriest, most arrogant and bigoted morons on the planet. This is how we produced Shakespeare and the Beatles. Our Wordsworths and Chaucers are nothing to be ashamed of, but Shakespeare was straightforwardly the greatest writer, in any language, in world history. It’s not just British people who think this; if anything, the Germans and Russians revere him more than we do. But what are the odds that the same country would have also produced history’s greatest pop band, four hundred years later but just a hundred miles away? This one damp island simply can’t stop generating the world’s mass culture. High fantasy, heavy metal, the computer, James Bond, Ziggy Stardust. The world’s universal sport is association football, which we invented. The second most popular is cricket, which we invented too. We are simply not like the other peoples of the world. The things we produce are simply better, better in a way no one else can really approach, which means our people must be far, far worse.

But lately, things seem to have stalled. Our last truly massive global phenomenon was _Harry Potter_. I don’t think the work is much good, but it was clearly right for the moment; around the world, there were billions of adults secretly waiting for permission to lap up a load of dreck for children, and JK Rowling gave it to them. Fine. It’s essentially the only thing this country currently has going for it; in most of our nicer and more scenic towns the only businesses still operating are knockoff _Harry Potter_ gift shops. But the first book came out thirty years ago; it belongs to the age of Britpop, Cool Britannia, the YBAs. Since then, we’ve produced absolutely nothing of note. If our country is going to survive the twenty-first century, we need another one.

We need to pursue our comparative advantage with a terrible ruthlessness. Nothing else matters; only art. We can let the industry rot, we can let our education system collapse, we can watch as entire towns depopulate in the north. Maybe the police will need to strategically withdraw from certain population centres. It doesn’t matter. As long as we average one _Harry Potter_-level global cultural phenomenon per generation, we’ll keep ourselves afloat. Wherever the rule of law still barely holds, there’ll be gift shops for tourists from richer countries to waddle around in.

But speaking of rich people—right now, only 18% of people in creative fields have a working-class background. The British working classes have an extraordinary creative genius, but right now immense talent is going unrealised because of the stranglehold posh dilettantes have over the arts. That needs to change. It must no longer be possible to think of the arts as a career for people who went to private school; we need to turn it into something like military duty, penal labour for the health of the state. Drop all the STEM subjects from schools. Kids don’t need to count; we should impress on them from early childhood that the only thing they should be doing at school is trying to start a band. Almost all those bands will fail. We just need one. As soon as our children learn to read, we should be chaining them up to typewriters like an infinity of monkeys, just in case one of them produces the next _Hamlet_. There will be a lot of dogshit. We just need one.

There is one more thing we can do. If my theory holds, and we all know it does, the UK’s failure to dominate global culture this century has an obvious explanation: the British have been getting nicer. Maybe it’s demographic change, maybe it’s the Northern Ireland peace process, maybe it’s the phones—but we are not as crass, tawdry, or bigoted as we ought to be. To save our arts, to foster a new generation of charmingly witty writers and brilliant, original musicians, we need to carry out the mass moral immiseration of the entire British population. I want tax breaks for people who abandon their children at the supermarket. I want fines on interracial friendships. I will make this place a hell, because I know that when it’s done someone on this island will produce a work of art so pure and perfect, so decent, so generous, so good-humoured, that just one single word could eternally justify England before the eyes of God.

_THE RESULT. The competition is finally over; Britain waits to discover whose boot it will suffer under for the next three years. The JUDGES are whispering. But on stage, something’s gone wrong. An argument about the 1905 Partition of Bengal has suddenly escalated; HAMZA HARAMZADA is grabbing RORY FITZWEATHER-REGRETTABLE by the lapels while RORY FITZWEATHER-REGRETTABLE ineffectually tries to bat him around the head with an open palm. Each of them is calling the other a cynical, greedy despoiler of the honest peoples of the world. FUCHSIA WALLOP dithers between them, at first trying to drag them apart, then lightly jabbing them in the eyes with her stubby fingers when neither of them pay any attention to her. The AUDIENCE is getting restive. Further brief sprays of tear gas fail to halt the murmuring. SAM KRISS rocks back and forth on his grisly haunches and lets out a long, plaintive howl. In the studio ceiling, there’s a sudden groan, the sound of rusted metal shuddering against itself. Unsettling clunks. Four or five new leaks appear, dousing previously well-sheltered sections of the AUDIENCE. And then, all at once, it caves in. An immense quantity of stagnant, algal gloop floods the studio. Suddenly the AUDIENCE and the CONTESTANTS are scattered in one great green churn. The bulletproof acrylic box containing KEIR STARMER is tossed on the waves of stinking rainwater that burst through the studio walls and out into the streets beyond. It’s not stopping. The waters rise through the streets as terrified people flee, flooding the vape shops and charity shops and dowdy little newbuild hovels on the edge of town, and spreading over the fields of England. The fens and the marshes ease themselves slowly into the mire. The hunched-up hills of Yorkshire unclench and are buried. The waters spread until, up in the Highlands, the peak of Ben Nevis is a little islet, and then a patch of ground in the waters, and then finally gone. All that’s left where the island of Britain once lay are blue waters. The waves quiet. The clouds begin to lift. Sunlight sparkles on the edges of the sea. And now the grey curtains of rain have lifted, someone sailing this bright new sea might spot something glittering in the far distance. A clear plastic box, bobbing on the surface, in which KEIR STARMER still sits, wringing his hands, with the expression of a man who’s just bit into a lemon. Oh dear, he’s muttering to himself, oh dear, oh dear._
